# What the 2022 winners looked like, whether our scanners caught the bottom, and whether multi-timeframe confluence works

**Date:** 2026-10-09 · **Status:** research result, not trading advice
**Charts and tables:** `docs/mtf_case_study/index.html` (46 weekly charts + summary tables) · **Scanner recall:** `docs/mtf_case_study/scanner_recall.md` · **Event study:** `docs/mtf_event_study_results.json`

## Bottom line

1. **The bottom is a market event, not a stock event.** 11 of the 43 stocks studied made their low in the same week, 2022-10-14 (ADI AMAT AMD ASML AVGO CIEN KLAC LRCX MPWR NVDA NXPI), the week of the Nasdaq low. Others lowed with the market on 2022-11-04, 2022-12-23, 2023-01-06 and (a second wave) 2025-04-04.
2. **At that low the winners looked ordinary.** Against every liquid stock that week, the median chip/AI winner ranked in the **14th-21st percentile** on every strength measure (13- and 26-week return, distance below the 30-week average, depth off the 52-week high, relative strength, RSI). The five user-named stocks were lower still (8th-16th). The only trait above the median was weekly volume (78th percentile: heavy volume at the low).
3. **No scanner of ours caught the bottom.** The first flag from any of them came after the low, typically **15-30 weeks later and +40% to +70% off it**, and each flagged hundreds to thousands of other stocks the same week.
4. **There is a confirmation sequence, and it is slow.** After the low (median of the 37 chip/AI names): volume expansion +7 weeks, weekly close above the 30-week average +5w (+37% off the low), monthly close above the 10-month average +12w (+45%), 30-week average turning up +15w (+43%), **first multi-timeframe confluence +16w (+48%)**, 26-week breakout +17w (+53%), relative strength at a 52-week high +38w (+74%).
5. **Confluence signatures do not select winners out of sample.** On 10 held-out market-recovery events, stocks matching four pre-registered multi-timeframe signatures did no better than all liquid stocks bought the same week with the same exit (excess return -0.04 to +0.04, every interval spans zero). They looked good in 2022, where I chose them (S3 +0.22, S4 +0.41), which is the usual in-sample result.
6. **"Let them ride" depends entirely on the exit.** From the first confluence, the median chip/AI winner returned **-6%** with a weekly-30-week-average exit, **+2%** with a monthly-10-month-average exit and **+46%** with a 30% trailing stop, capturing only ~17% of its run. Even among the best winners of five years, the exit decided the result.

## 1. The case study: what the winners looked like (descriptive, chosen after the fact)

43 names (the user's AMD NVDA MSFT AMZN TSLA, 25 chips, 12 AI-infrastructure names, META GOOGL AAPL) plus SPY, QQQ, SMH. The "run" is each name's best trough-to-peak on weekly closes since 2021-10-08, **chosen after the fact, so nothing here is evidence of an edge**.

| | low week | run | drawdown inside the run | below its 2-year high at the low |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| NVDA | 2022-10-14 | x20.8 | -36% | -66% |
| AMD | 2022-10-14 | x11.3 | -59% | -64% |
| MU | 2022-12-30 | x22.7 | -54% | -49% |
| AVGO | 2022-10-14 | x10.5 | -40% | -36% |
| TSLA | 2023-01-06 | x4.3 | -48% | -72% |
| AMZN | 2022-12-30 | x3.3 | -28% | -55% |
| MSFT | 2022-11-04 | x2.4 | -23% | -35% |
| SPY / QQQ / SMH | 2022-09/10 | x2.2 / x2.9 / x7.6 | -17% / -21% / -34% | |

MSFT, AMZN and TSLA are not runners of the same kind as the chips: x2.4 to x4.3, close to the index. The AI-leaning chip and infrastructure names ran x5 to x74 (nine names above x30: APP, SNDK, CLS, VRT, CRDO, SMCI, WDC, PLTR, LITE), while the analog and legacy chips (TXN, NXPI, QCOM, MCHP, ADI, ON) ran only x2.3 to x3.6, close to the three mega-cap names. Six names have short histories (APP, SNDK, CRDO, NBIS, ALAB, ARM), so their drawdown-into-the-low figures are partial.

Inside the run the winners were hard to hold: the median name fell 43% from a running peak (middle half of names 32% to 51%). That is the practical meaning of "let them ride": a trailing exit tighter than roughly 35-60% shakes out most of these names.

## 2. Would our scanners have caught the bottom? No

Each scanner was replayed week by week (completed bars only) over all 10,980 stocks, and for each name the first week it flagged within [low - 8 weeks, low + 52 weeks] was recorded, with the price versus the low and how many other liquid stocks it flagged the same week (a precision proxy). 43 names, excluding the three benchmarks:

| Scanner | names flagged in window | median first flag | median price vs the low | other stocks flagged that week | flagged before the low |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Finder `signal` (golden cross, compression) | 38 | +17 weeks | +46% | 666 | 1 |
| Finder `maker` | 5 | +15w | +65% | 101 | 0 |
| Finder `pennyking` | 1 | -8w | +1% | 6 | 1 |
| Turnaround rule (bottom + trigger) | 1 | +31w | +300% | 3 | 0 |
| Textbook "compression + golden cross" | 43 | +7w | +31% | 927 | 9 |
| Textbook "stage 2 trend" | 38 | +32w | +69% | 492 | 0 |
| Textbook "tight base breakout" | 18 | +32w | +43% | 194 | 1 |
| 17 learned capitulation rules (rejected earlier) | 9 (any of them) | | | | |
| **New:** B0 above the 30-week average | 43 | +5w | +40% | 1,490 | 11 |
| **New:** S1 RS leader | 41 | +19w | +47% | 418 | 1 |
| **New:** S3 breakout leader | 30 | +30w | +71% | 56 | 0 |
| **New:** S4 full confluence | 25 | +31w | +94% | 46 | 0 |

Reading it: the scanners that fire early (compression + golden cross, B0) fire on a large share of the market (12% and 50% of all stock-weeks), so catching the winners is not information. The selective ones (S3, S4, about 1-2% of stock-weeks) fire 30 weeks after the low, at +70% to +94%. No scanner both flagged the winners early and kept the flagged group small. The earlier learned capitulation rules fired on only 9 names, mostly APP (11 of 17 rules) and META (10 of 17).

## 3. Does confluence across timeframes work? The out-of-sample event study

**Question.** After a market-level confirmation, do stocks matching a multi-timeframe signature, bought at the next open and ridden, beat all liquid stocks bought the same week with the same exit?

* **Events:** SPY weekly close back above its 30-week average after at least 8 weeks below it with a 15%+ drawdown: 13 events (2001-05 to 2025-05); 10 held-out and mature (all except 2022-08, 2022-11, 2025-05). SMH events (17) are reported separately.
* **Signatures, written down before the run** (Finviz-style, in `setup_engine/mtf.py`): B0 above the 30-week average (market beta); S1 top-20% 13-week relative strength above a rising 30-week average; S2 30%+ below the 52-week high, above the 30-week average, top-20% 26-week strength; S3 26-week breakout on 1.5x volume, above a rising 30-week and a 10-month average; S4 = S3 plus relative-strength-line new high plus the index above its 30-week average.
* **Exit (primary, fixed):** 30% trailing stop on weekly closes, filled at the next open, with a -35% hard stop, a 156-week cap and 1% cost. Moving-average exits are reported as exposure-confounded: they eject stocks that start below the line, so they flatter a signature that requires being above it.
* **Statistics:** control = mean of every liquid stock that same week; bootstrap clustered by event, 98.75% intervals (Bonferroni over four signatures); verdict on held-out events only.

| Signature | trades | stocks | events | mean return | excess vs control [98.75%] | events positive | ran 5x in 3y (sig / all) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| B0 (beta) | 6,672 | 2,045 | 10 | +22.5% | +0.004 [-0.018, +0.022] | 5/10 | 1.9% / 2.3% |
| S1 RS leader | 1,539 | 1,070 | 10 | +22.1% | -0.008 [-0.070, +0.061] | 5/10 | 3.0% / 2.3% |
| S2 beaten-down reclaim | 251 | 234 | 10 | +42.0% | -0.040 [-0.178, +0.185] | 3/10 | **8.0% / 2.3%** |
| S3 breakout leader | 200 | 194 | 10 | +18.1% | +0.035 [-0.085, +0.169] | 5/10 | 2.5% / 2.3% |
| S4 full confluence | 129 | 127 | 9 | +14.7% | +0.031 [-0.091, +0.195] | 5/9 | 2.3% / 2.3% |

**Verdict: no demonstrated edge for any signature.** S1-S3 fail the first test (interval above zero) and the majority-of-events test; S4 passes the majority test (5 of 9) but not the interval test. Every survivorship-adjusted figure is negative. That last test is a deliberately high bar (the signature's missing-stock share is assumed 1.5x the market's at -100%); it is not what decides the result, because every signature already fails before it.

**What the table says.** Mean returns of +15% to +42% per trade are almost entirely market recovery: the control earns the same. S2 is the one different shape: its stocks reached 5x in three years 3.5 times as often as the average liquid stock (8.0% vs 2.3%), yet its excess return is slightly negative, because the other 92% lose enough to cancel it. That is a lottery-ticket profile, not an edge.

**In-sample versus out-of-sample.** On the 2022 events, where I looked at NVDA and the other winners when choosing the signatures, S3 showed +0.22 and S4 +0.41 excess. On the 10 held-out events they show nothing. A placebo run (40 random non-event weeks) shows no systematic excess either, so the signatures are not a hidden bottom-timing rule.

**Independent check.** Recomputing B0 and S1-S4 from the saved rows with plain pandas reproduced the trade counts, stock counts, mean returns, excess returns and positive-event counts exactly.

## 4. Group level (descriptive, run after the event study, not pre-registered)

Stocks cannot be told apart at the confirmation, but the group can be timed. The same SMH events, entered at the next open after the event week:

| exit | positive events (of 17) | note |
|---|---:|---|
| 30-week average (R1) | 2 | exits within 1-4 weeks most times: entry is the first close just above the line, so the next wobble crosses it |
| 10-month average (R2) | 3 | same whipsaw |
| 30% trailing stop (R3) | 12 | median +41%, mean +36% |

With the 30% trail, SMH was positive in all 10 events from 2009-03 onward (+2% to +99%; 2025-05 is still open at +161%) and positive in only 2 of the 7 events from 2001-2008 (the failures lost 23% to 36%). SPY, same exit: positive in 9 of 13, with 2001-05, 2001-12, 2008-05 and 2019-02 losing 18% to 36%. This is one ETF, 17 events, in one long uptrend: it supports "trade the group's confirmation with a wide trailing exit", not any stock selection.

## What this means for a starting point

* The market/sector confirmation (SPY or SMH weekly close above the 30-week average, after a 15%+ drawdown) is the technical event that mattered. For SMH in 2022 it came 4 weeks after the low and 28% off it; the median stock's own 30-week reclaim came 5-6 weeks after its low and 37% off it. That still left x5 to x20 for the AI-leaning leaders.
* Technicals did not tell NVDA from the thousands of similar beaten-down charts, at the low or at the first confirmation. What separated them was relative strength that emerged later (at a 52-week high 38 weeks after the low), and by then the winners were already up 70%+.
* If the stock is picked on the AI story (your part), the technical part is timing and risk: enter on the market/sector confirmation, expect drawdowns of -35% to -60% inside the run for chips, and treat the exit as the main decision. A 30% trail rode SMH but sold AMD at +45% in mid-2024 on a name that went on to x11.

## Caveats

* **The winners are survivors and the case study selects on the outcome.** Every number in sections 1-2 is descriptive. Section 3 is the test, and it is survivor-only too: the delisted stocks are missing, so every mean is an upper bound.
* **The effective sample is small:** thousands of trades but ~10 independent market moments, in about three regimes (2001-03, 2008-11, 2019-20). Event-clustered intervals with 10 clusters are crude.
* **2022 informed the signatures** and is excluded from the verdict; the 2025 event is immature (reported, not decisive). The cohort was named by you and by me from knowledge of who won.
* **Seen before the full run:** a 3-batch smoke run of each script (meaningless sample). No parameter was changed after any result. Section 4 was added after the event study.
* **Data:** benchmarks (SPY, QQQ, SMH, TSM, ASML, ARM) come from Yahoo, clipped to the cache's last date (2026-10-06). Prices are split-adjusted, not dividend-adjusted.
* **Not wired yet:** the new weekly/monthly filters work in code (`Screen` with `indicators_mtf`), but `scripts/run_screen.py` still uses the daily-only table.
